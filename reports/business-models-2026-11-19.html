How the business works

Every e-commerce business answers two questions: whose brand is on the product, and who holds the stock and ships it. Below is each answer drawn out from your side, then the money for each one, run through the repo's scripts.

The two questions

Whose brand?
Theirs: resell or affiliateYours: private label

With your own brand, you set the price and build something you can sell later. With theirs, you're a middleman.

Who holds and ships the stock?
Supplier (dropship)You (from home)A warehouse company (3PL)Amazon (FBA)

Farris's "3PL" means third-party logistics: a warehouse you pay to store your stock and ship each order. It's like Amazon FBA, but for orders from your own website.

What this shows: a cheap repeat product loses money on the first order when you buy customers with ads, and only earns it back months later if they reorder. That works for brands with cash to wait. With $1,800, a $149 product that pays for itself on order 1 is safer.

Your questions, answered straight

Am I storing and shipping it myself?

Only for the first 5 kits, so you can check quality. After 10 happy customers, a 3PL warehouse ships for you and Amazon ships Amazon orders. You never pack thousands of boxes.

Wouldn't repeat products be better?

Yes, eventually, which is why the plan adds door guards and paw wipes for the same customer. But the chart shows cheap repeat items lose money at first. The math only works with a bundle ($69 skincare set: roughly break-even over a year) and strong retention.

Partner with a brand, or my own brand?

Your own brand. Reselling someone else's product leaves about $4 per $25 sale before ads, the brand sets your price, and you compete with their own store and Amazon. Affiliate works only with free content.

Supplements and K-beauty from US suppliers?

US on-demand makers (Blanka, Supliful) do ship direct with no stock, and quality is easier. But supplements are out of scope in our rules (FDA and FTC claim risk). Skincare is allowed but crowded, and it's a different customer, so it would be a separate brand.

Quality on a big, heavy product?

A real risk. That's why the first order is only 5 kits, paid through escrow, and you test kit 1 before anyone buys. Nothing goes live until it passes.

No repeat buys, so it's all ads. Is that OK?

It's the trade-off. A $149 price leaves room to pay for ads and still profit on order 1 at bulk costs. Add-ons, the two-car pack and Amazon (where ads cost less) raise what each customer is worth.

People don't think to buy it.

True, so it sells through video that shows the problem (dog sliding into the footwell) and the fix. Our earlier research found about 5,500 sold per month on Amazon at $130+, so the demand exists.

Ruff Liners is strong.

They prove the market. We compete on price ($149 vs $169.99), a design without window inserts, and a fit guarantee. If the 5-kit test shows nobody buys from us, we stop at a small loss.

My pick

Own brand, tested small, then a 3PL and Amazon. For this budget, a high-price product that profits on the first order is the safest path.
