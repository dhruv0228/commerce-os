## 1. Who, budget and rules

- **Owner:** Dhruv Patel, a software person in Florida. The business runs as a DBA under his existing LLC, Patel Software Enterprises LLC. He does not appear on camera; ads would use paid creators, plus an AI-twin voiceover that never claims to have used the product.
- **Budget:** $1,800 total, with no second tranche; Dhruv later said "$2,000 or less".
  - Planned split:
    - Store and software $100
    - Samples and shipping $250
    - Creative $100
    - First ad test $400
    - Supplier working capital $300
    - Refund reserve $250
    - Contingency $400
- **Goal:** first sales as fast as possible, December allowed. Then scale to $10k+/month profit.
- **Milestone 1:** one product with verified supplier quotes, an approved sample, positive contribution before ads, and 10 customers who get it on time with no refunds.
- **Stop rule:** after 2 failed product tests in a row, stop and review.
- **House rules:**
  - Every fact carries a label: VERIFIED, ESTIMATED, ASSUMED or UNKNOWN.
  - All math runs in scripts, never in prose.
  - Supplier messages are drafted by the AI and sent only by Dhruv.
  - Nothing is published, bought or contacted without Dhruv's OK.
- **Out of scope:**
  - China-direct parcels to customers
  - Supplements, medical, CBD, vapes, kids' products, fragile goods
  - Health claims
  - Copying patented designs
  - Reselling other brands as the hero product (allowed only for add-ons)
- **Premium rules:**
  - A physical sample reviewed before any page or ad goes live
  - US delivery in 2 to 8 business days, proven by a test order
  - Blind shipping (no supplier invoices in the box)
  - Written defect terms
  - A backup supplier before scaling
- **Financial rules:**
  - Pre-ad contribution = revenue minus all variable costs except ads.
  - Break-even CAC = pre-ad contribution. Target CAC = 75% of break-even.
  - Pass only if the category's benchmark cost per purchase is at or below the target CAC. Also check a "tough" case at benchmark x 1.3 (new-store penalty).
  - Kill rule: pause an ad with zero purchases once its spend reaches break-even CAC.
  - Scale rule: raise budget by at most 30% per 48 hours.

## 3. Product research history

  - **Sell:** Davie Fogarty's playbook (section 13).
- **Dash cam:** rejected. A 4K kit lands at about $79 to $80 (37.5% tariff) and loses money at $105 to $129.

  - Ruff Liners runs about 1,133 active Meta ads (checked Nov 18).

## 5. The kit (product bible; all specs ASSUMED until the sample)

- **Platform:** a rigid 3-panel hard-bottom platform that bridges the footwell.
  - Standard size about 52 x 25 in; XL about 58 to 60 x 28 in.
  - Target: under 10 mm of sag at 150 lb.
  - Folds to 20 in or less.
- **In the box:** 2 strap-on door guards, a seat-belt tether with a metal swivel, a rubber fur tool, a storage bag and a fit card.
- **Shipping box:** about 9 lb, 20 x 14 x 5.5 in.
- **Claims allowed only after the sample proves them:**
  - "Hard bottom that doesn't sag" (needs a filmed kneel test)
  - "Fits most cars, SUVs and trucks"
  - Waterproof or washable
  - A measured weight rating
- **Claims we never make:**
  - Crash safety
  - Anxiety or health claims
  - "Patented", "#1"
  - Fake strike-through prices (FTC 16 CFR 233)
  - Unearned reviews (FTC 16 CFR 465 and 255)
- **Guarantee drafts:** fit guarantee (we pay the return), 30-day returns, 1-year warranty. All ASSUMED until the supplier's defect terms are in writing.

- **Message format** (research-based, about 130 words):
  - Intro: "Dhruv, founder of Barkfare, a US dog travel brand."
  - Plan: a 5-unit express trial to Florida, then 50 to 100 units.
  - Must-haves: rigid board that doesn't sag, folds to about 20 in, 2 separate strap-on door guards.
  - Six questions: price at 5/50/100 units, will they do 5, express cost and days with duties paid, logo minimum and cost, production time, factory or trading company.
  - Leave out: target price, "startup", the home address.
  - Round 2 (later): blind shipping, defect terms, carton size, payment method.
  - Also post one Alibaba Request for Quotation.
  - Send in the Florida evening (morning in China). Follow up once after 3 days.
  - Expect: about half reply and 2 or 3 good answers. Example: Jungle Scout's 14 of 21 replied, 3 were good.
- **Channels research:**
  - Direct messaging on Made-in-China and Alibaba plus one RFQ is the best route for 5 units.
  - Sourcing agents cost $100+ per order, so they're for bulk.
  - CJdropshipping's US warehouse needs about 100 stocked units.
  - Zendrop, Spocket and Syncee only resell catalog items.
  - US makers have minimums of 25 to 500+.
  - Faire is other brands only.
  - Free customs records (ImportYeti) find competitors' factories.
- **Verification checks:**
  - Pay only through escrow (Trade Assurance or Secured Trading), with the spec written into the order.
  - Never wire money off the platform.
  - The "Verified" badge proves the company exists, not that it makes the product. Check the Chinese business register (gsxt.gov.cn) or Qichacha.
  - Red flags: off-platform payment, changed bank details, a payee name that doesn't match the company, a price far below the others.
  - Third-party inspection costs about $200 to $350 a day; worth it only for 50+ unit orders.
  - Trade Assurance is mediation, not a guaranteed refund; disputes are reportedly allowed for about 30 days after delivery (ESTIMATED).
- **Tariffs and landed cost:**

## 9. Business models explained

**Fulfillment (who holds and ships the stock):**

| Model | How it works | Cash up front |
|---|---|---|
| Dropship | Supplier ships direct to the customer | $0 |
| Ship from home | You store, pack and mail by USPS; only the first 5 kits | About $404 |
| 3PL | A warehouse company stores your stock and ships each order (about $3 pick and pack plus postage). Like Amazon FBA, but for your own website. This is how Ruff Liners works | 150 kits about $6,976 |
| Amazon FBA (Farris's model) | Amazon stores and ships, keeps about 15% plus a fee, and pays every 2 weeks. Ads are cheaper, but Amazon owns the customer | Same stock |

**Brand (whose name is on it):**
- Resell a brand: thin margin, the brand sets the price.
- Affiliate: commission only, works with free content.
- Private label: your own brand, our model.
- Made on demand by a US maker (Blanka, Supliful, Rocktomic): your label, no stock.

**The "777" list:**
- It is Anton Kraly's free list of "777 high-ticket dropshipping niche ideas" (Drop Ship Lifestyle), a lead-in to his paid course ($1,497 to $10,497 reported).
- Related names: Trevor Fenner (Ecommerce Paradise), Dropship Breakthru (the golf simulator story; also a standing desk brand that did $1M in sales with zero profit).
- The FTC sued a different course seller, Ecommerce Empire Builders, in Sept 2024 over false income claims.

**High-ticket mechanics:**
- Supplier approval requires an LLC, EIN, resale certificate, a finished store, a phone, sometimes $1M/$2M insurance.
- Approval takes 2 days to 4 weeks; about 10 to 20% of applications succeed.
- Margins are 15 to 35% gross and 7 to 20% net. MAP fixes the price. The supplier is paid by card when the order comes in.
- Shopify and Stripe may hold reserves (for example 10% for 120 days).
- Freight damage averages about 1.2%. Customer return freight runs $750 to $1,800 for a sauna, with 15 to 50% restocking fees.
- Teachers' own advice: $3k to $10k to launch, a $2k to $5k ad budget, first sales in months 3 to 6.

**Consumables facts:**
- **Rocktomic** (US supplements): plans $97 to $297/month, $2 per item, ships in 1 to 2 days. Creatine costs $14.97 (suggested price $35.97) while Optimum Nutrition sells at $17.97 on Amazon.
- **Supliful:** $69/month, fulfillment $1.99.
- **Blanka** (US skincare, Dallas):
  - Hyaluronic acid serum $14.90 (suggested $35), cleanser $15, moisturizer $28.70.
  - Flat $9.99 shipping per order.
  - Made to order takes 8 to 14 business days, which fails our 2-to-8-day rule unless stocked.
  - Amazon hyaluronic acid serums sell for $6 to $10.
- **K-beauty wholesale:** COSRX snail essence $9.26 wholesale (Amazon $15.99); Anua toner $15.14 (Amazon $19.69). There's counterfeit risk, and Korean sunscreens count as drugs in the US.
- **Regulation:**
  - Supplements need FDA notification and FTC evidence; insurance about $2.8k to $4.5k a year.
  - Cosmetics fall under MoCRA: the brand owner is the "responsible person", with a small-business exemption under about $1M; insurance about $1k to $3k a year.
