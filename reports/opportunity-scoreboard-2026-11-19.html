Which business should you start with $2,000?

Three kinds of e-commerce, four ways the money moves, and 24 real opportunities scored out of 100. Every number below comes from scripts/opportunity_rank.py, built on research checked today.

Three kinds of e-commerce

Small repeat products

Supplements, skincare, K-beauty. $16 to $89.

  • Cheap to start, no stock
  • Ads cost more than the first order earns
  • Profit depends on reorders

Best here: Own-brand serum (US maker), score 50

Own brand, mid-ticket

Barkfare extender kit. $149.

  • Small first order (5 kits)
  • Profits on the first sale at bulk cost
  • One-time purchase, add-ons for more

Best here: Dog back-seat extender kit (Barkfare), score 72

High-ticket dealer

Saunas, golf sims, fireplaces. $600 to $9,000.

  • No stock, big profit per sale
  • Expensive clicks, few buyers
  • One order can cost more than your budget

Best here: Electric fireplaces, score 54

How the money moves

Four business models, drawn from your side. Farris's Amazon model is the first one.

Amazon marketplace

Farris's model
Factoryyour productSea freight5 to 7 weeksAmazon warehousestores + shipsBuyerPrime, 1 to 2 days

You buy stock up front. Amazon takes about 15% plus a shipping fee per sale, and pays you every 2 weeks. Buyers are already searching, so ads cost less, but Amazon owns the customer and cheap copies show up fast.

Own brand, your store

Barkfare
Factoryyour logoExpress, then sea5 kits, then 150Home, then 3PLyou ship, then they doBuyer2 to 5 days

You own the brand, the price and the customer list. You pay for stock before it sells, and every sale comes from ads or content.

High-ticket dealer

the 777 model
Your storedealer for a US brandYouorder + pay supplierUS brand warehousefreight or parcelBuyer1 to 3 weeks

No stock, but you pay the supplier ($1,000 to $7,000) when the order comes in, sometimes before Shopify pays you. MAP fixes the price, so every dealer sells the same item at the same price.

Made on demand

No stock and almost no cash to start. Low prices mean ads cost more than the first order earns, so you profit only when people reorder.
