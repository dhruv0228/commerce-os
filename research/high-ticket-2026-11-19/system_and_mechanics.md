# High-ticket dropshipping: the "777" system and the real mechanics (2026-11-19)

Research date for every source below: 2026-11-19 unless another date is given. Labels follow CLAUDE.md section 5. No supplier quote or platform data was collected here, so nothing in this file is VERIFIED. Course-seller claims are marked [COURSE SELLER].

## Summary

1. **Who "777" is (high confidence).** "777" is almost certainly Anton Kraly's free list, "777 high-ticket dropshipping niche ideas," from Drop Ship Lifestyle. He gives it away for signing up to his free training, and the training sells his paid course (the Drop Ship Blueprint and coaching). His own blog post (dated Oct 28, 2026) offers "my list of 777 high-ticket dropshipping niche ideas," with a sidebar titled "FREE: MY 777 HIGH-TICKET DROPSHIPPING NICHE IDEAS FOR 2027." Kraly also wrote a book, *Drop Ship Secrets* (2018, ISBN 9780692173329). That book is not "the 777 book." 777 is the niche list or eBook given away with the training. I found no "777 method," no "7-7-7 rule" and no book titled "777."
2. **Close candidates to rule out.** Trevor Fenner of Ecommerce Paradise sells *The High-Ticket Dropshipping Blueprint* (2nd ed., 2026, 630+ pages, $9.99), but his free list has "1,000+" niches, not 777. Ben Knegendorf and Jon Warren of Dropship Breakthru / The Dropship Podcast have the "golf simulators, $11M a year" story. "Ecommerce Empire Builders" (Peter Pru) is a different seller, which the FTC sued in Sep 2024 (details in section 7).
3. **How the model works.** You apply to US brands or distributors to become an authorized online dealer. You list their products at the MAP price. When a customer pays you, you pay the supplier, usually by card at the time of the order, and the supplier ships direct, often by LTL freight. Traffic is mainly Google Shopping and Search. Phone and chat matter.
4. **Numbers.** All of these are ESTIMATED and most come from course sellers. Gross margin is 15-35%, usually quoted as 20-30%. Net margin after ads is 7-20%. Restocking fees run 15-30%. Dropship fees run $0-25 per order. Conversion rate is roughly 0.3-1.5%, with no primary benchmark found. The sales cycle is 7-30 days. Practitioners report first sales in month 1 to 6. Fenner, a course seller, says "$3,000 to $10,000 to launch properly," with $1,000 to $2,000 "possible but slower."
5. **Fit with our constraints.** The fit is poor. A single $3,000-5,000 order means paying the supplier before Shopify pays us, and new Shopify Payments accounts can be held or put on a reserve (Shopify documents reserves of 10% for 120 days, for example). Google Shopping budgets that course sellers themselves recommend ($2,000-5,000) exceed our whole $1,800. Suppliers want a live, professional, niche-matched store, a domain email and often insurance, and the best brands in saunas, golf simulators and solar are reported to be closed to new dealers. Reselling other brands as the hero also breaks CLAUDE.md section 1 unless Dhruv changes that rule.

## 2. Supplier approval

Requirements most often cited (ESTIMATED; Ecommerce Paradise [COURSE SELLER] about Oct 2026, and Dropshipping Champions, Jul 10, 2026, an affiliate site):
- A formed entity (LLC) and an EIN. Sole proprietors are sometimes accepted, at lower approval rates.
- A state sales tax permit and resale certificate. Some suppliers need the certificate valid in their warehouse's state.
- A live, finished website with real category and product pages, policy pages, a phone number and an email on the store's domain. Placeholder or "Coming Soon" sites are rejected.
- A business address and phone. Fenner says a residential address plus a personal cell plus a Gmail address "in combination" is disqualifying.
- A business bank account in the entity's name.
- A certificate of insurance for "roughly a third" of serious industrial brands, typically "$1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate," with the supplier named as additional insured.
- Sometimes a credit application (for net terms) or a minimum annual purchase. Fenner cites VEVOR's application asking for a "$5,000 plus" annual budget.
- Some brands only sell through dealers with a physical showroom or a stocking commitment.

How long it takes (ESTIMATED, Fenner [COURSE SELLER]):
- EIN: immediate.
- LLC: 1-15 business days.
- Sales tax permit: same day to 4 weeks. For Florida, registration is free on Form DR-1, and the Annual Resale Certificate (DR-13) is mailed after approval. One vendor says online registration takes 3-5 business days.
- Website: 2-6 weeks.
- Supplier decision: "two to ten business days" for online applications, or "two to four weeks" when a phone call is required.
- Reapplying after a rejection: "six to eight weeks."
- Practitioner pattern: build the store first (about a month), then apply (Jayden Clark on My Wife Quit Her Job ep. 662).

Approval odds (ESTIMATED, self-reported): Jayden Clark expects "about 10% to 20%" of suppliers to say yes and "roughly 7 in 10" to decline. Some brands only work with stores that have run 3 to 6 months. He applies across 50-100 products to get 5-10 suppliers. No independent approval-rate data was found (UNKNOWN).

Rejection reasons cited:
- Wrong or missing documents, for example a resale number for the wrong state or insurance below the required limit.
- A free email address.
- An empty site, or a site that doesn't match the niche you claim.
- No phone number or address.
- Territory caps on online dealers.
- A policy against dropshipping.
- No sales history.

## 4. Traffic, conversion, sales process

- **Channel.** Google Shopping and Search are the main channels in every source, with Bing Shopping second. Fenner recommends "One-Campaign-Per-Brand PMax." Jayden Clark gets about 80% of paid traffic from specific commercial searches and targets 10-20x ROAS (self-reported). Epps (older): 5-10x ROAS on Google Shopping vs about 3x on Facebook (FLAG OLD, self-reported).
- **CPC** (DataForSEO, US, checked 2026-11-16, in `research/high-ticket-2026-11-16/google_ads_keywords.json`): golf simulator $4.50, infrared sauna $5.92, outdoor sauna $4.28, cold plunge $9.71, golf simulator package $7.02. All are HIGH competition. These are ESTIMATED Google Keyword Planner figures; real Shopping CPCs are UNKNOWN until tested.
- **Search benchmark.** WordStream/LocaliQ 2026 (13,000+ US campaigns, Apr 2025 to Mar 2026) gives Home & Home Improvement a $8.33 CPC, 8.05% CVR and $90.92 cost per lead. Their "conversion" mostly means service leads, not $3,000 purchases, so these numbers do not transfer to ecommerce (ESTIMATED, use with caution).
- **Conversion rate for $1,000+ items.** No primary benchmark was found (UNKNOWN). Secondary figures (ESTIMATED):
  - Fyresite (Jan 30, 2026, no primary source cited): 0.3-1.5%, and 0.3-0.8% above $3,000.
  - DTCpages (18 Shopify stores, 2026): median 1.27% for stores at $100+ AOV vs 2.64% under $60.
  - TopGrowthMarketing (33 DTC stores): median 1.05%; the lowest performers were high-ticket stores.
  - Fenner's own sales page assumes 1% [COURSE SELLER].
- **Sales cycle.** 7-30 days, and 2-4 weeks with multiple sessions above $3,000 (Fyresite, no primary source cited); another guide says 3-14 days (ESTIMATED).
- **Phone and chat.** No source gives a share of orders closed by phone (UNKNOWN). Jayden Clark: 20-50% of purchases involve at least one touch point, and he tracks click-to-call ads as conversions. Epps: about 90% of questions are answered by the landing page, and the rest go to the supplier's rep. Fenner calls phone sales "a big chunk." A business phone costs about $15-30/month (Fenner).
- **Google learning period.** Google says conversion data drives the length of the learning period. At a handful of orders per month, a high-ticket store gives Smart Bidding very little data, which a Shopify Community thread and agency blogs report as stalled or volatile delivery (ESTIMATED).

## 5. Cash and risk

- **Payout holds and reserves** (Shopify Help Center, checked 2026-11-19):
  - Reserves are either a fixed amount (example: "$1,000 held for 120 days") or a percentage (example: "10% held for 120 days").
  - Listed triggers include "industries with long delivery timelines" and "sudden volume surges."
  - During a payout hold, customers can still check out, but the merchant isn't paid until it clears.
  - For high-value goods, banking partners may require more information.
  - Shopify does not publish a rule about AOV for new stores. Third-party guides and community threads say a single unusually large order on a new store can trigger review, and report holds of weeks, in one case 120 days (anecdotal).
- **Stripe** (its own guide): rolling reserves "usually 5%-15%" for a rolling period "e.g., 180 days" for new or high-risk businesses (ESTIMATED).
- **What this means for cash (inference, not a figure).** The dealer pays the supplier at the time of the order, by card. Customer money may arrive days later, or be held. Each order needs card limit or cash equal to the supplier cost, and the money spent on ads before the sale is gone either way.
- **Chargebacks.** No rate data for this model was found (UNKNOWN). Fenner flags e-bikes as high chargeback risk [COURSE SELLER]. Practitioners claim the model has fewer chargebacks than China dropshipping, because a real brand ships quickly (self-reported). A $3,000+ dispute is a large share of a small store's cash.
- **Freight damage and returns:**
  - LTL is typical over about 150 lb, with curbside delivery by default. Liftgate and inside delivery cost extra.
  - Customers must note damage before signing for delivery, or claims "get dramatically harder" (Dropshipping Champions).
  - Average LTL damage rate is about 1.24% of shipments (Flock Freight 2025 shipper survey, 1,000 shippers, ESTIMATED). Other sources give 1-2%.
  - Return costs are high. Jayden Clark reports a 1-2% return rate at $200-500 per return. Heavenly Heat Saunas says return freight "typically" runs $750-1,500. Sun Home Saunas says $1,500-1,800 round trip.
  - Restocking fees on retailer policy pages: saunas 20-25% (some sellers), Salus Saunas 50%, Sunlighten a flat $499. Golf: SkyTrak 20-40%, The Indoor Golf Shop 15%, Omaha Sports & Games a minimum of 15%. Customers usually pay return freight.
  - These are the policies retailers show customers. What a supplier charges its dealer on a return or damage is in each dealer agreement and is UNKNOWN until we see one.
- **Sales tax.**
  - The resale certificate lets the dealer buy from the supplier without paying sales tax.
  - Florida: register on DR-1 (free) and receive the Annual Resale Certificate DR-13.
  - Remote-seller threshold: $100,000 of taxable remote Florida sales in the prior calendar year (Florida DOR page). As a Florida-based seller we would collect on Florida deliveries from the start, because we have physical presence in the state (inference; confirm with the DOR or an accountant).
  - Other states: economic nexus thresholds apply as sales grow (UNKNOWN for us; not researched here).
  - The state rate (6%) and county surtax were not confirmed from an official page in this pass (UNKNOWN).
- **FTC Mail Order Rule.** Ship within the stated time, or within 30 days if no time is stated, even though the supplier does the shipping (CLAUDE.md section 9).

**Failure modes reported:**
- Supplier rejections because the store looks unfinished.
- Ad spend with zero sales. Several Shopify Community threads report spending hundreds on Google Ads with no sales.
- Clicks that are too expensive to learn from: a low-volume store "will get a few clicks and after a month you'll still have no idea."
- Revenue without profit. Knegendorf's standing desk brand did $1M in sales with "zero profit."

- Frozen payouts.
- Freight returns that wipe out a sale's margin.

## 7. Honest critique and course-seller bias

- Almost every specific number in this niche (margins, ROAS, timelines, "$11M a year," "$50 million in student results") comes from people who sell courses, communities, done-for-you stores or supplier directories. None of it is audited.
- Kraly, Fenner, Knegendorf/Warren and Jayden Clark each sell education or services. Even the "independent" reviews (Ippei, DreamGrow, TxtCart, LaunchpadReviews) promote competing programs or earn affiliate commissions. Ippei's review flags Drop Ship Lifestyle's Trustpilot reviews as "Potentially Manipulated."
- **FTC enforcement.** On Sep 18, 2024, the FTC sued Empire Holdings Group LLC, d/b/a Ecommerce Empire Builders (Peter Prusinowski), in E.D. Pa. (Case 2:24-cv-04949). It alleges false earnings claims ("$10,000 per month") and says "Defendants understate the actual costs required to run and maintain a dropshipping business, especially ongoing marketing costs," that its stores "often lose money, even the stores Defendants tout as success stories," and that refunds required clients to "prove ... they took specific steps." The complaint alleges at least $14.3 million taken from consumers since 2021. The FTC has brought other earnings-claim cases, such as Automators and Publishing.com ($1.5M settlement). This case is not about Kraly or Fenner. It shows the pattern to discount, especially refund guarantees that depend on finishing tasks, which Drop Ship Lifestyle also uses.
- **What the model really is.** It is an online retailer of other companies' brands, with little moat. Steve Chou (MWQHJ host) said he is "usually not a fan of dropshipping," though he largely agreed with Clark's branded-dealer version. You win on ads, content, phone service and site trust, not on product or price.
- **The math needs volume we cannot fund.** At about 1% conversion and $4-10 clicks, each sale needs roughly 100 clicks on average. That is an illustration, not a forecast, and it must be run through `scripts/economics.py`. The 2026-11-16 illustration in `research/high-ticket-2026-11-16/calc.json` used the old Amboras 1% platform fee and must be rerun with Shopify's `payment_pct` 0.029 before anyone relies on it.

## 8. Implications for Barkfare (for Dhruv's decision; no decision made here)

- Reselling other brands as the hero conflicts with CLAUDE.md section 1 ("never the hero"). Doing this needs Dhruv to change the rule.
- Course sellers' own recommended ad budgets ($2,000-5,000) are larger than the full $1,800 budget. One supplier payment of $1,700-4,000 at the time of the order, with a possible Shopify payout hold, exceeds the $300 working-capital line (planning allocations, CLAUDE.md section 0).
- Suppliers' requirements overlap with steps we already need: the LLC and DBA exist, and product liability insurance is already required before the first sale. A domain email, a business phone and a Florida resale certificate are new items.
- The niches Dhruv heard of (saunas, golf simulators) are the ones Fenner calls most oversaturated, with the hardest supplier access for new dealers.
