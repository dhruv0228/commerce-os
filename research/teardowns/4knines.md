## Positioning and product strategy

- **Message:** "The last seat cover you'll ever need" (lifetime warranty), and the door guards that don't fight you.
- **Competitive marketing:** it names the complaint category (window inserts) and quantifies it with its own research.
- **Catalog:** a broad seat-cover range by vehicle (crew-cab, cargo liners, bucket seats). The extender is a new entrant.

## Visible weaknesses

1. Few reviews on the extender (182).
2. Returns cost the buyer money unless they bought protection.
3. The slowest delivery promise of the set: 5 to 7 days.
4. A sitewide 22% sale that reads as permanent.
5. "Seams and zippers are not watertight". Not for captain's chairs or heated seats.
6. The infographic gallery looks like an Amazon listing.

## Economics (public)

$132.59 sale or $169.99 list, free shipping over $99. All else is UNKNOWN.

## What we should learn

- **Adopt:**
  1. Research-backed positioning with dated sources. We can run our own sag test and fit study, honestly sourced.
  2. Fit quiz plus vehicle icons next to add to cart.
  3. Collecting the vehicle at email signup.
  4. FAQ that answers practical objections (slipperiness, hair).
  5. A stated "not for" list, which builds trust.
- **Not for us:**
  1. A wide seat-cover catalog.
  2. Automaker supplier credentials.
  3. Charging for return labels.

The price at capture ($132.59) undercuts our $149, so our kit must show more in the box and a better fit promise, not a lower price.
