## 1. What templates do credible operators and sourcing professionals recommend?

### Takeaway
Every credible template uses the same skeleton: a one or two line business introduction, the product (link or photo plus a few hard specs), then a short numbered list of commercial questions (price at two or three quantities, MOQ, sample cost and time, production lead time, logo/customization, factory or trader). They differ mainly on how much to put in the first message. Most are aimed at Amazon private label; none is DTC-specific, but the content transfers directly.

### Cited Findings

**Jungle Scout, Greg Mercer, "Million Dollar Case Study" (Mar 17, 2017; Amazon private label; OLDER, flagged)**
- Structure: short personal and professional introduction (Greg introduces himself and his company, says they want to buy baby hooded towels for the US market, sold mainly online), an expected annual volume stated as contingent on a trial order and sample evaluation, a bulleted spec list (size, hood design, material, softness/thread count, finishing, colour), "a pitch on why they should care," then "a list of very specific questions" — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)
- The five questions: (1) photo, specifications and an EXW quotation; (2) manufacturer or trading company; (3) lead time for samples and for a trial order; (4) can a logo be added and where; (5) sample cost including express shipping to the USA — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)
- Method: copy and paste the template into each listing's "Contact Supplier" box, or mark suppliers as Favorites and message them together from the Messages portal; include photos to overcome language barriers; contact 10 to 20 suppliers with the same template and record responses — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/how-to-find-alibaba-suppliers/), [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)

**Jungle Scout, Greg Mercer, "Collaborative Private Label Launch" (Oct 27, 2015; Amazon private label; OLDER, flagged)**
- Structure: greeting and intro (name, company, purpose); product specs (pack count, dimensions, end style, food-safety needs) with the instruction "Include as many specifics as possible"; numbered questions on sample cost and shipping, sample timing, packaging, per-unit cost with air shipping, PayPal for full orders, manufacturing lead time; close asking for shipping and other policies and whether a sample order can be discussed — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/our-collaborative-private-label-launch-finding-a-manufacturer-on-alibaba/)
- Signed as "Purchasing Agent" to signal a larger organization; used a separate Gmail account for outreach; budgeted about $50 to $75 per sample; messaged 14 factories — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/our-collaborative-private-label-launch-finding-a-manufacturer-on-alibaba/)

**SellerSprite Team, "How To Reach Out To Alibaba Suppliers Like A Pro" (June 16, 2026; Amazon sellers; current)**
- Structure: greet the contact by name; introduce yourself, job title, company, and whether you sell in the US or EU; name the product with its Alibaba link; list five specs (material, size or weight, colour options, logo placement, packaging); numbered questions on tiered pricing at three quantities, MOQ including customization, sample and production lead times, sample cost with express shipping, shipping to a US ZIP (DDP if possible), certifications; close by saying you are contacting several suppliers, will pick a partner in about 2 to 3 weeks, and want long-term repeat orders — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)
- Length: "A strong first message is usually 120 to 180 words, plus a short numbered question list." Attach 1 to 3 files at most (reference photo, optional logo, packaging example) — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)

**Supplyia (sourcing agent blog, Mar 17, 2026; general importers; current). Close to verbatim:**
- Basic Inquiry Template — [Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/?p=3168):
  > Subject: Interested in [Product Name]
  > Hello [Supplier Name],
  > My name is [Your Name], from [Company Name]. We are interested in your [Product Name] and would like to learn more details.
  > Could you please provide the following:
  > - Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ)
  > - Price per unit (for different order quantities)
  > - Customization options and associated costs
  > - Sample costs and shipping methods
  > Looking forward to your reply.
  > Best regards,
  > [Your Name]
- Customized Product Inquiry Template — [Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/?p=3168):
  > Subject: Custom [Product Name] Inquiry
  > Hello [Supplier Name],
  > My name is [Your Name], from [Company Name]. We are looking to customize [Product Name] with the following specifications:
  > Dimensions: [Details] / Material: [Material Requirements] / Packaging: [Packaging Preferences] / Branding: [Logo/Branding Requirements]
  > Please provide pricing and production timelines.
  > Looking forward to your cooperation.
  > Best regards, [Your Name]
- Supplyia warns that suppliers' salespeople see the same Alibaba template repeatedly, so personalise it — [Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/?p=3168)

**Cosmo Sourcing, Jim Kennemer (sourcing agent; dated "Sep 19", year not shown; Amazon/FBA-leaning)**
- Subject line: "I'm interested in [Product Name]" or "RFQ for [Product Name]." Body: introduce yourself and company, ask for a quote — [Cosmo Sourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/questions-to-ask-suppliers-on-alibaba)
- First-message request list (seven items): product photos plus a photo of a specific detail (forces them to shoot real photos for you); MOQ; price at MOQ and at one other quantity (to gauge flexibility); price of any customization; cost to ship a sample; shipping terms; closest port (should match a factory's claimed location) — [Cosmo Sourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/questions-to-ask-suppliers-on-alibaba)

**Sourcing Pioneer, Alan Xiao (sourcing agent founder; updated Aug 19, 2026; general/beginner buyers; current)**
- Template asks the supplier to confirm: can they make the item as described and have they made similar; unit prices at 100, 500 and 1,000 pieces; their minimum order and "whether a smaller trial order is possible"; sample cost and sample lead time; full production lead time; "Are you the factory or a trading company?" The buyer states a decision timeframe of about two weeks and attaches a reference photo — [Sourcing Pioneer](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/)
- Why three tiers: a small price drop across tiers suggests a reseller; a steep drop suggests a real production line — [Sourcing Pioneer](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/)

**Alibaba.com official buyer guidance (undated page)**
- Three-part structure: (1) introduce yourself and your business (what it does, your role, where it is based; complete your profile; use a professional business email); (2) describe the product in detail (specs, packaging, safety, branding, quantity, photos); (3) ask specific vetting questions (price at certain volumes, certifications if applicable, available stock, shipment origin, shipment dimensions and weight) — [Alibaba.com](https://activity.alibaba.com/ggs/how_to_send_an_effective_inquiry_on_alibaba.html)
- Tip: "Share everything you can in your first inquiry to a seller," because time zones make drip-fed details slow — [Alibaba.com](https://activity.alibaba.com/ggs/how_to_send_an_effective_inquiry_on_alibaba.html)

**SaleHoo, Simon Slade (Apr 10, 2025; dropshippers and wholesale buyers)**
- Suggested intro line: "My name is [your name] and I'm the product manager of [your company/website]." Introduce yourself as a business, not an individual — [SaleHoo](https://www.salehoo.com/blog/why-dont-suppliers-reply-to-my-emails-and-how-to-fix-it)

**Global From Asia/Anywhere (Jan 12, 2018; general importers; OLDER, flagged)**
- "A typical inquiry should contain a short introduction about yourself or your company," then questions on MOQ, pricing, shipping costs and policies, payment methods — [GFA](https://globalfromanywhere.com/getting-factory-responses)

### Inferences
- Audience split: Jungle Scout, SellerSprite, Cosmo Sourcing and Helium 10 material target Amazon private label (FBA prep, ports, EXW/FOB, bulk sea freight). Supplyia, Sourcing Pioneer, Alibaba and SaleHoo are generic. None targets a DTC brand that needs single-order US delivery, so a Barkfare message must add the US-specific questions these templates omit (US warehouse stock, ship time to a US address, blind shipping), which CLAUDE.md section 2 requires.
- The question set is remarkably stable across 2015 to 2026 sources: price at tiers, MOQ, sample cost and time, production lead time, logo/customization, factory vs trader.

### Gaps
- No verifiable first-message text from Matt Clark / Amazing.com, Kevin David, Davie Fogarty, Jake Kalanik, Gabriel St-Germain or Sebastian Ghiorghiu was found (course material is paywalled or video-only; YouTube transcripts were not fetched).
- Jungle Scout's current gated template and Leeline's template body could not be read.

## 2. How long should the first message be? One short message or everything up front?

### Takeaway
Most practitioners say short: roughly 120 to 180 words plus a numbered list of about 5 to 7 questions. There is a real split: Alibaba's own guide says put everything in the first message to beat time zones, while several sourcing agents say suppliers answer only a few points, so keep the first list short and hold the rest for round two.

### Cited Findings
- "A strong first message is usually 120 to 180 words, plus a short numbered question list"; avoid a long paragraph without attachments — [SellerSprite, Jun 2026](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)
- Jungle Scout's template is "a short personal and professional introduction" plus "a list of very specific questions" (five questions) — [Jungle Scout, 2017](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)
- Cosmo Sourcing keeps the first list to seven items so suppliers are not overwhelmed, and moves other questions to a second round — [Cosmo Sourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/questions-to-ask-suppliers-on-alibaba)
- Global Sources: the first email should give detailed product requirements in a short message because factory salespeople are unlikely to read long documents (search snippet; page itself returned HTTP 429) — [Global Sources](https://www.globalsources.com/knowledge/why-good-chinese-suppliers-don-t-reply-to-your-emails-rfqs-and-how-to-fix-that/)
- Suppliers often respond to only a few points in a long email, so hold some questions for follow-up; use simple sentences, digits and bullet points (search snippets, Maple Sourcing guides) — [Maple Sourcing](https://www.maplesourcing.com/connect-with-china-suppliers-step-by-step-process.html)
- Contradicting view: Alibaba says "Share everything you can in your first inquiry to a seller," since holding details back causes delays across time zones — [Alibaba.com](https://activity.alibaba.com/ggs/how_to_send_an_effective_inquiry_on_alibaba.html)
- Sourcing Pioneer expects "two rounds of questions" as normal before comparing quotes — [Sourcing Pioneer, Aug 2026](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/)
- Clarity over cleverness, because many suppliers are not native English speakers — [Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/?p=3168), [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)

### Inferences
- The two camps reconcile as: give complete product specs up front (so they can price), but limit questions to the 5 to 7 that decide whether to continue. That fits Dhruv's preference for short, business-only messages.

### Gaps
- No source tested message length against reply rates; the 120 to 180 word figure is one vendor's guideline, not measured.

## 3. Which questions first, which later?

### Takeaway
First: price at two or three quantities, MOQ (and whether a smaller trial order is possible), sample cost and lead time (with express shipping to the US), production lead time, logo/customization, and factory or trading company. Later (round two, with shortlisted suppliers): payment terms, packaging details, certifications, bill of materials, FOB/EXW/DDP detail, carton weights, sample-fee refund on bulk orders, export history.

### Cited Findings
- First round per Cosmo Sourcing: photos (including a specific detail), MOQ, price at MOQ and one other quantity, customization price, sample shipping cost, shipping terms, nearest port — [Cosmo Sourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/questions-to-ask-suppliers-on-alibaba)
- Second round per Cosmo Sourcing: payment terms and options, bill of materials, shipping methods and cost terms, FOB quote, production lead time, sample terms, catalog, years in business, logo and packaging options, EXW/FOB/DDP, carton weight and dimensions, units per carton, FBA prep. Shortlist round: past US exports, certifications, trade insurance, new-customer payment terms, sample-fee refund on a full order — [Cosmo Sourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/questions-to-ask-suppliers-on-alibaba)
- Jungle Scout's first five: EXW quote with photo and specs, manufacturer or trader, sample and trial-order lead times, logo, sample cost with express to USA — [Jungle Scout, 2017](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)
- SellerSprite puts certifications and DDP shipping to a US ZIP in the first message — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)
- Leeline: ask MOQ in the first email so you do not invest effort in a supplier whose MOQ you cannot afford (search snippet) — [Leeline](https://leelinesourcing.com/contact-vendors/)
- Sourcing Pioneer: before ordering, get the full deal in one message (what the price covers, packaging, production time, payment split, a quality standard tied to the approved sample) — [Sourcing Pioneer](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/)
- Do not negotiate price before specs are locked — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)

### Inferences
- For Barkfare the first-round list should swap "nearest port / FOB" (bulk sea-freight questions) for "US warehouse stock and ship time to a US address", because the 5-kit trial and the dropship model need US delivery, and blind shipping is a hard rule. Payment terms, certifications and packaging artwork can wait.

### Gaps
- No source ranks questions by their effect on reply rate.

## 4. What NOT to say: first order, tiny quantities, target price, personal details

### Takeaway
Do not lead with "I'm a small startup" or "this is my first order", do not ask for a quantity far below a stated MOQ without acknowledging it, and do not give a target price in the first message. Operators frame a small first order as a "trial order" tied to a stated, realistic follow-on quantity, and offer to pay a higher unit price for it. Some advise quoting your scale quantity in the quantity field; this can tip into overpromising.

### Cited Findings
- Introducing yourself as a small startup signals small orders and low-price demands, which suppliers avoid — [GFA, 2018](https://globalfromanywhere.com/getting-factory-responses)
- Asking for a tiny quantity below a stated MOQ signals you have not done your homework (search snippet) — [Maple Sourcing](https://www.maplesourcing.com/connect-with-china-suppliers-step-by-step-process.html)
- Sourcing Pioneer: "Ask directly for a trial order." Explain you will reorder if the product sells and offer a slightly higher unit price for the smaller run; many suppliers agree because a small first order that becomes a repeat customer beats no order — [Sourcing Pioneer, Aug 2026](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/)
- Jungle Scout stated an expected annual volume "contingent on a trial order and sample evaluation" — [Jungle Scout, 2017](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)
- SellerSprite: Alibaba auto-fills the supplier's MOQ in the quantity field; replace it with your realistic scale number (example 1,000 units) even if the first order is smaller, to signal long-term potential — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)
- SaleHoo: small quantities may not attract interest; be realistic about volume; some suppliers go below MOQ for promising buyers — [SaleHoo, Apr 2025](https://www.salehoo.com/blog/why-dont-suppliers-reply-to-my-emails-and-how-to-fix-it)
- Paying more for a lower MOQ or signing a forecast agreement are common tactics; supplier-side Alibaba blogs describe trial orders at a higher price per piece (search summary of Alibaba seller blogs, promotional) — [Alibaba Seller Blog](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/other-apparel/trial-order-moq-flexibility-guide-alibaba-b2b)
- A supplier refusing single-unit or very small orders is a normal reply; move on (search snippet) — [Sourciavera](https://sourciavera.com/blog/buying-one-item-retail-alibaba-what-happens)
- Target price: request quotes without giving a target, then share one if offers are far off — [QualityInspection.org](https://qualityinspection.org/target-price-chin/). Global Sources' expert: mention the target AFTER the initial quotes come back; a dissenting expert notes a stated target can help in China because quality ties to price — [Global Sources](https://www.globalsources.com/knowledge/should-i-give-my-suppliers-a-target-price/) (snippet only)
- Avoid subjective spec words like "high quality" (search snippet) — [Global Sources](https://www.globalsources.com/knowledge/why-good-chinese-suppliers-don-t-reply-to-your-emails-rfqs-and-how-to-fix-that/); avoid spam-trigger subject words such as "urgent", "dear friend", "order now" — [GFA](https://globalfromanywhere.com/getting-factory-responses)
- Attachments conflict: SellerSprite and Jungle Scout recommend 1 to 3 photos — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro); some agents say attachments in a first cold email make suppliers cautious (email context, not Alibaba chat; snippets) — [JustChinaIt](https://www.justchinait.com/?p=10906), [QualityInspection.org](https://qualityinspection.org/initial-sourcing-phase/)

### Inferences
- A compliant, non-overpromising phrasing for Barkfare: state the trial and the honest next step, e.g. "We would start with a 5-unit trial order, then 50 to 100 units if the trial goes well," and put 50 or 100 (not 1,000) in Alibaba's quantity field. Quoting 1,000 would be overpromising for a $1,800 budget.
- Personal details (home address, ZIP, personal phone) are not needed in a first message; the US state or "US market" is enough to price shipping. No source recommends sharing them up front; this also matches CLAUDE.md keeping the ZIP out of the repo.
- On Alibaba's own chat, 1 to 2 product photos are low risk; the "no attachments" advice applies to cold emails sent to factory addresses.

### Gaps
- No source measures whether saying "trial order" lowers reply rates. Evidence is practitioner opinion.

## 5. Company name, website, email signature: do they matter?

### Takeaway
Yes, in every source that addresses it: introduce yourself as a business with a name and role, and use a business email domain. No source quantifies the effect on reply rates.

### Cited Findings
- Alibaba: introduce your business, role and location; complete your profile; use a professional business email — [Alibaba.com](https://activity.alibaba.com/ggs/how_to_send_an_effective_inquiry_on_alibaba.html)
- SaleHoo: introduce yourself as a business, not an individual; buy a domain (about $12) for a yourname@yourcompany.com address; optionally build a website; add a few lines about your business — [SaleHoo](https://www.salehoo.com/blog/why-dont-suppliers-reply-to-my-emails-and-how-to-fix-it)
- Greg Mercer signed as "Purchasing Agent" to signal a larger organisation, though he used a separate Gmail account — [Jungle Scout, 2015](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/our-collaborative-private-label-launch-finding-a-manufacturer-on-alibaba/)
- SellerSprite's template includes your job title, company and market (US/EU) — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)
- Leeline: set up DMARC so emails land in the inbox, not spam (snippet) — [Leeline](https://leelinesourcing.com/contact-vendors/)

### Inferences
- For Barkfare: sign with "Dhruv Patel, Founder (or Purchasing), Barkfare" and mention the US market. A personal Gmail works (Greg Mercer used Gmail) but a brand-domain address is the stated best practice once the domain exists. Calling Dhruv "Purchasing Agent" for a one-person company is a mild misrepresentation; "Founder" or "Sourcing" is honest.

### Gaps
- No data on reply-rate lift from a company website or domain email.

## 6. Mass-sending, reply rates and reply speed

### Takeaway
Yes, operators send the same (lightly personalised) message to 10 to 30 suppliers. Published reply rates are anecdotal: about 67% in Jungle Scout's 2017 case (14 of 21), and a vendor's claim of 30 to 60% as "normal". Only a few replies are usable (3 of 21 in Jungle Scout's case).

### Cited Findings
- Jungle Scout: contacted 21 factories; 14 responded (about 67%); 3 were worth following up (about 14%); canned replies with irrelevant info and no genuine interest were ignored; good replies answered every question (ANECDOTAL, single case, 2017) — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)
- Jungle Scout 2015 case: 14 factories messaged; "numerous responses" with no count (ANECDOTAL) — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/our-collaborative-private-label-launch-finding-a-manufacturer-on-alibaba/)
- Contact 10 to 30 suppliers; "In many categories, a 30 to 60 percent reply rate is normal" (vendor claim, no dataset, so ANECDOTAL); example funnel: message 20, shortlist 6, sample 3, pick 1 — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)
- Contact 10 to 20 with the same template — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/how-to-find-alibaba-suppliers/); message all 10 to 15 shortlisted suppliers — [Sourcing Pioneer](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/); consult at least half a dozen — [Cosmo Sourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/questions-to-ask-suppliers-on-alibaba)
- Alibaba's Messages portal lets you message favourited suppliers in one blast — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)
- Personalise the template because salespeople see the same ones repeatedly — [Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/?p=3168), [Cosmo Sourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/questions-to-ask-suppliers-on-alibaba)
- Speed: Alibaba tells sellers to respond within 24 hours (and a Korean seller guide cites a target of under 12 hours average) — [Alibaba Learning Center](https://seller.alibaba.com/learningcenter/content/detail/PXCUO23A.htm), [Alibaba seller guide (KR)](https://seller.alibaba.com/kr/usage-guidelines/respond-to-inquiries-and-messages). SellerSprite treats a 90%+ response rate and replies within 24 hours as a green flag, below 70% as a warning (rule of thumb) — [SellerSprite 2026 guide](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/amazon-fba-alibaba-supplier-guide-2026)
- Send during Chinese office hours (about 9am to 6pm China time) so messages are not buried — [Sourcing Pioneer](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/); avoid Chinese New Year and National Day holidays — [GFA](https://globalfromanywhere.com/getting-factory-responses)

### Inferences
- For a 5-unit trial, expect a lower useful-reply rate than these bulk-buyer examples, since some factories will decline small orders. Sending to about 15 to 20 shortlisted listings to get 3 to 5 usable answers is consistent with the sources.
- Timing note for Dhruv (Florida, US Eastern): China office hours roughly equal 8pm to 5am Eastern; sending late evening Eastern lands at the start of the China workday.

### Gaps
- No measured, multi-buyer reply-rate dataset exists in the sources found. No data on reply rates for very small (under 10 unit) trial requests.

## 7. Follow-up: when and how

### Takeaway
No authoritative cadence exists. Practitioner advice: a short polite nudge after about 2 to 3 days, a second try within a week (possibly by WhatsApp or phone if offered), then move on. Re-ask unanswered questions plainly and rephrase if language is the barrier.

### Cited Findings
- If no reply within a week, send a polite follow-up; if that fails, try phone, messaging apps, or a sourcing agent — [Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/?p=3168)
- Follow up within 2 to 3 days when a supplier needs time; send a short note asking whether the question was received (search summary of sourcing guides) — [Owl Sourcing](https://owlsourcing.com/chinese-suppliers-ignore-your-inquiries/)
- A follow-up phone call shows you are serious — [GFA](https://globalfromanywhere.com/getting-factory-responses), [SaleHoo](https://www.salehoo.com/blog/why-dont-suppliers-reply-to-my-emails-and-how-to-fix-it)
- Suppliers often say "yes" even when they cannot; treat "we'll try" as no guarantee; rephrase questions and press for firm answers or move on — [Cosmo Sourcing](https://www.cosmosourcing.com/blog/questions-to-ask-suppliers-on-alibaba)
- A Google Doc template sequence uses a follow-up email "3 days later" (author unknown; example only) — [Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1AlbgaanYAX6aRrj0u1llr3msSXNjDu4Os-pGVro2tnI/export?format=pdf)
- Stating a decision window (about 2 to 3 weeks, or about two weeks) in the first message creates a natural deadline — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro), [Sourcing Pioneer](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/)

### Inferences
- A workable cadence for Barkfare: day 0 first message; day 2 or 3 one-line nudge on the same Alibaba thread listing unanswered question numbers; day 6 or 7 final nudge; then drop. Phone calls are out of scope for a short-message, business-only approach.

### Gaps
- No source tested follow-up timing against outcomes.

## 8. Moving to WhatsApp, WeChat or email: pros and risks; Trade Assurance

### Takeaway
Many suppliers answer faster on WhatsApp or WeChat, and moving there is not itself a scam signal. But Trade Assurance protection depends on ordering and paying through Alibaba, and Alibaba's dispute process relies on its own chat and order records. Operators advise: chat anywhere if needed, but put specs and agreements in the Alibaba order and pay on platform.

### Cited Findings
- Many suppliers are more responsive on WhatsApp/WeChat; WhatsApp is common in Chinese trade; moving there is not itself a scam; the risk is a seller asking you to order or pay outside Alibaba; red flags include avoiding platform monitoring, requesting direct payment, fake invoices, changed bank details. Complete, unedited WhatsApp screenshots can support a Trade Assurance dispute on a covered order — [Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/?p=3168)
- Many buyers move to email or WeChat after the first reply for files and details; start on Alibaba and keep clear written agreements; pushing off-platform payment immediately is a red flag — [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/How-To-Reach-Out-To-Alibaba-Suppliers-Like-A-Pro)
- Keep the deal on platform: create the order there, pay there, keep messages in the message centre; attach your spec and sample photos to the Trade Assurance order; a claim has to be on the order to count; "payment outside Alibaba" is the most common way money disappears; never pay 100% up front; never agree to under-declare customs value — [Sourcing Pioneer, Aug 2026](https://sourcingpioneer.com/the-ultimate-alibaba-for-beginners-guide/)
- Alibaba's seller blog says that in disputes Alibaba can only consider evidence from its official chat system; off-platform or third-party payments are not protected — [Alibaba Seller Blog](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/b2b-trade/trade-assurance-complete-guide-alibaba-secure-payment), [Wise](https://wise.com/gb/blog/alibaba-trade-assurance)
- Conflict on evidence: Supplyia says WhatsApp screenshots can support a dispute; Alibaba's seller blog says only official-chat evidence is considered. Refund windows also conflict across sources (15 vs 30 days after delivery) — [Supplyia](https://www.supplyia.com/?p=3168) vs [Alibaba Seller Blog](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/b2b-trade/trade-assurance-complete-guide-alibaba-secure-payment)
- Alibaba itself now pushes WhatsApp notifications to suppliers for buyer messages (Korean seller guide) — [Alibaba seller guide (KR)](https://seller.alibaba.com/kr/usage-guidelines/respond-to-inquiries-and-messages)
- Greg Mercer moved promising suppliers to Skype after initial screening (2017) — [Jungle Scout](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/evaluate-alibaba-suppliers/)

### Inferences
- Safest rule for Barkfare: keep the first messages and every price, spec and term on Alibaba chat; if a supplier moves to WhatsApp, copy any agreed terms back into the Alibaba thread and the Trade Assurance order; never pay off-platform. Sharing a WhatsApp number exposes a personal phone, so use a business number if any.
